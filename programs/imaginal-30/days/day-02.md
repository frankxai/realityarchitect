# Day 2 — The Atlas

## Today's intent (planned)

Look at your whole life once, in twelve domains, and choose at most three to work on this month. Coverage, not twelve projects.

## The practice

1. Open `reality/atlas.md` (Studio: Atlas). Under its heading, start a table: the header row `| Domain | Now | Wanted | Priority |` and the divider `| --- | --- | --- | --- |`.
2. Add one row per domain, in this order: Body & Vitality, Mind & Mastery, Heart & State, Character & Code, Spirit & Source, Love & Union, Lineage & Legacy, Circle & Community, Wealth & Sovereignty, Craft & Contribution, Sanctuary & Lifestyle, The Golden Age.
3. Score each twice, from 0 to 10: how it is now, and where you want it. Go fast; first answers are fine. A row like `| Craft & Contribution | 5 | 9 | |` is enough. A blank means not looked at yet, not a failure.
4. Look at the gaps. Write `yes` in the Priority column for at most three domains: the ones you are willing to work on now. A large gap is not automatically the right one.
5. Check that each priority has a heading below the table with one `- True now (reported): …` line. Add any that are missing.

## Why it works

**Meaning.** Think of your life as a house with many rooms. Some you live in; some you rarely enter. The scores are not grades. They say how each room feels to you today. Choosing a few is also care for the rest: you decide what gets your attention, and let the others wait without guilt.

**Mechanism.** A gap between now and wanted gives effort a direction. Naming it beside the present is the first half of mental contrasting, which supported follow-through in experiments better than picturing the wanted state alone. Limiting priorities protects attention, which is finite. The scores are self-reports: useful for seeing your own view change over time, not a measurement of your life.
